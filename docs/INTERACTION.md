# Sofatap single-screen interaction

## The phone is the touchpad

The main iPhone screen is one large rounded touch surface. The named device selector and a dedicated question-mark guide button sit inside its top edge; the microphone and left-hand keyboard control sit inside its bottom edge. The resting surface has no instructional text or separate mouse-arrow graphic. Connection state appears beside the current device name with an accessible label. Tapping that selector opens a device-management bottom sheet; the question mark opens an illustrated guide. QR scanning is part of adding a device in the management sheet. Desktop receivers use the approved Mint Calm design described in [receiver design](RECEIVER-DESIGN.md).

- Slide one finger to move the computer pointer.
- Tap once anywhere for a left click.
- Double-tap with one finger within the physical left half for **two left clicks**, or within the right half for **two right clicks**. A right-half double-tap must not first send a left click. These halves stay physical left/right in every interface language and exclude the controls and edge-scroll strip. Only the guide illustrates their positions; the real touchpad adds no divider or text. This describes current source; build 15 retains its earlier double-tap mapping.
- Hold still for **300 ms** to press the left mouse button, then move to drag. On a computer the pointer starts moving after 3 points, but a press that rolls up to 7 points and then rests for 100 ms before the deadline still becomes a hold. A finger that leaves 7 points, or is still sliding at the deadline, only moves the pointer. Lifting the finger ends the drag. Cancellation, loss of control and leaving the app release the held button; the receiver independently releases owned input on disconnect.
- Slide two fingers to scroll, or slide one finger along the scroll strip inside the touch surface’s right edge. Two fingers drag the content: an upward swipe reveals content farther down the page, and a downward swipe reveals earlier content. The edge strip follows scrollbar direction: a downward swipe scrolls the page down. These gestures intentionally emit opposite wheel deltas for the same finger movement. The strip reserves a 44-point hit area below the guide control and within the pad, separate from the editor and action buttons, stops on lift/cancellation, and does not report the remote document position.

Pairing remains an occasional sheet, rather than a second everyday control mode. A text composer appears inline below the touch surface only when needed. The circular mint Send button sits inside the editor panel on the right, in a separate fixed-width column so text, its caret and the native scroll indicator cannot overlap it. Smaller muted Clear, computer Backspace and keyboard-toggle icons sit below the input box on the left, without backgrounds, while retaining 44-point touch targets. The arrow points down while the keyboard is open and up while it is hidden; the touch-surface keyboard icon tracks the same state. Both actions provide a selection haptic, a short press response and a state transition, with reduced motion respected. Hiding the keyboard keeps the composer and its toggle visible. The Send control remains present when temporarily unavailable; only the text editor scrolls. Pending live updates show progress in the same circular control. No character counter, duplicate keyboard toolbar or permanent live-input capability caption is shown. Notices share the action row on the right and appear only for actionable connection, protected-field, loading, composition or delivery states. Existing uncertain-delivery and manual resend safeguards remain available in that composer.

## Touch feedback

The microphone has a distinct pressed state: it compresses to 90% and darkens on touch-down, then restores over 160 ms on release. Its 64-point hit target stays fixed. Reduce Motion keeps the size fixed and uses an immediate brightness change. Valid start, stop and cancel taps request a light haptic; stopping or cancelling releases the recording audio session first. The feedback acknowledges a button action, while the existing recording icon and color report the actual microphone state. Disabled or cancelled presses do not start dictation, and automatic interruptions do not request this tap haptic.

A gently moving dot matrix fills the touch surface, including its resting state. Touch locally displaces and bends nearby dots; dragging strengthens the deformation, and release lets the field settle back. Single and double taps keep distinct expanding ripples that also perturb the field. These effects visualize the phone gesture, not the computer pointer's position or confirmation that a desktop application acted on it. Tapping the device selector, guide, microphone or keyboard control must not also send a mouse gesture.

Effects remain inside the touch surface. Reduce Motion keeps the matrix static and uses simpler touch feedback. App inactivity, detachment and disconnect pause ambient animation. Touch cancellation clears the local deformation and releases any held mouse button while the active field can keep drifting. Screen-reader labels describe the icon controls without adding visible instruction text to the surface. The text composer and pairing sheet retain the wording needed to review drafts, errors and connection details.


## Saved devices and languages

The management sheet stores multiple desktop, Android/Google TV and LG webOS pairings. Each row has a device type, name, local address and explicit active/connected state, plus rename, disconnect and local removal actions. Credentials and names remain in a device-only Keychain collection; the prior single-pairing record migrates after a successful atomic write. Removing the active device never selects another device automatically. Removing this iPhone's record does not revoke another phone or stop the receiver.

The build 15 source gives Add Device one **Scan QR Code** entry. Its upper section is a live camera preview; the lower section explains Mac, Windows, Linux and TV receiver requirements. Users do not choose a computer or TV type before scanning. Scanned codes, pasted links and incoming pairing links share the same parser: the URI identifies the desktop or Android TV protocol, while optional `platform` and `name` fields label the candidate. Those labels are display hints, not authentication. Only a successful certificate-pinned, credential-checked connection advances to the naming step. Users can keep the suggested name or save a local name and change it later in My Devices. LG webOS remains a separate manual IP and TV-approval flow from the same page; it does not support QR pairing. Android/Google TV remains experimental and has no qualified physical model or third-party app.

Build 15 remains compatible with the published receiver 0.7.0 beta.1's four-field desktop codes (`host`, `port`, `token`, `fingerprint`). The new desktop receiver source emits six-field codes by adding `platform` and `name`; these require iPhone build 15 or newer. The updated receiver binaries have not been released. Builds 13–14 keep the older Add Device → Computer entry, and build 12 uses the top-right QR icon. The current distribution status is recorded separately from this source-level interaction change.

Selecting a target stops dictation, releases gesture state, clears queued commands and ends the prior live editing session before connecting. Pending callbacks cannot acknowledge or replay input against the next target. Retained text requires explicit review before reuse on another computer; old delivery-prefix receipts are discarded. A user-confirmed promotion of imported remote text becomes a persisted local draft only at that confirmation boundary.

The iPhone interface and permission prompts support the [same 12 languages as the website](APP-LANGUAGES.md). Arabic follows native RTL layout; the touch-surface scroll strip remains at the physical right edge. On-device speech availability remains dependent on iOS, device and recognition language and is separate from interface-language coverage.

## Voice and keyboard input

An explicit microphone tap requests the iPhone's microphone and Speech permissions and starts local speech recognition when supported. A second tap stops capture. Audio stays on the iPhone. In a supported live field, partial recognized text and later corrections synchronize while the user speaks; stopping keeps the already synchronized text. In the fallback composer, partial text stays in the local draft until Send. Neither mode appends an extra Enter or form-submit action.

If local recognition is unavailable or permission is denied, the same composer accepts the iPhone keyboard, system dictation or Typeless. The app does not use a private API to start keyboard dictation. Backgrounding, interruption and cancellation stop capture. In live mode, confirmed IME text, Backspace, selection changes and Select All → Delete edit the imported existing computer text. In fallback mode, selection and deletion edit the unsent local draft. Keyboard Backspace on an empty fallback draft sends a Backspace key to the selected computer only when no sent-text receipt is retained; clearing the local draft resets that receipt. The explicit computer Backspace icon addresses the computer’s focused text input without changing the phone draft, including a nonempty fallback draft. It is unavailable during composition, speech, uncertain delivery, paused editing, dragging or a reported protected/non-text target. Users must select the intended computer text field first. These remote key actions must remain separate from clearing the local draft and must never be automatically retried after uncertain delivery. A confirmed delivery stays visible and editable: after sending A, appending B sends only B on the next tap. Edits to an already delivered prefix require an explicit whole-draft resend or a fresh draft, because fallback cannot replace computer text. A focus change or reconnect never imports an old field snapshot over an unsent continuation. Send flushes the latest native editor value before taking its snapshot and never commits an unfinished IME candidate. A live ACK confirms native text/selection readback; a fallback ACK confirms system event submission. Neither proves application save or form submission.

The receiver reports a small focus hint: `editable`, `other`, `protected` or `unknown`, plus a connection-local revision. The phone automatically opens the keyboard on a new editable target, waiting until an active drag is released so the keyboard does not interrupt it. Dismissing the keyboard keeps it dismissed for that same target. It does not automatically start recording. Protected or unknown targets never automatically open input UI. The manual keyboard remains available when a target does not expose accessibility information.

Background focus detection uses native accessibility metadata and does not read the field value, selection, window title or password. Opening the phone editor starts a separate live-session request that reads the supported field's complete text and selection. The imported live value stays in memory; the fallback draft remains locally saved. Windows live editing is limited to native Edit/RichEdit controls; Mac requires writable AX selected text and selection range. Unsupported browser/custom controls, Linux and older receivers use review-and-send. See [focused-field detection and data handling](TEXT-FOCUS.md).

Live editing is the default where supported. A focus, text or selection conflict, disconnection or uncertain write pauses it and never causes automatic replay. The user checks the computer and explicitly reopens editing. Native replacements touch only the changed span; surrounding text and formatting are preserved. This is distinct from a promise of compatibility with every input field.

## Free TV control is the next feature

The [product roadmap](ROADMAP.md) separates free manual control from paid voice automation. Extend the manual touch surface to televisions before integrating an agent runtime. Verify genuine pointer movement, click and scrolling where the TV exposes them. Directional focus navigation is a different capability; expose that mode honestly on TVs that only support remote keys. TV right-click, held-button dragging, text editing and power behavior require their own capability checks and hardware acceptance.

The development LG webOS pointer preview reuses the touch surface with a separate gesture profile: tap selects immediately, two-finger movement scrolls, and Back/Home replace unavailable TV text controls. Long presses do not hold a mouse button, and double taps are two selections rather than a right-click. The native pairing sheet supports manual private IPv4 entry and TV approval; discovery and TV text input remain pending. See the [technical plan](research/tv-implementation-plan.md).

Keyboard input and speech-to-text dictation remain basic input features. A microphone used to enter words must never implicitly start an automated task or require a Pro subscription.

## Later paid voice-controlled computer actions

After free TV control, the paid feature is a standalone computer-use service: spoken command → intent/planning → explicitly authorized observation and bounded mouse/keyboard execution → progress or clarification on the phone. Qualify Windows, macOS and Linux first; paid TV automation follows the desktop version. It must run as part of Sofatap's own product, independently of Codex. The current app and receiver already communicate directly; Codex is a development tool, not their runtime.

The [October 13 architecture research](research/natural-language-pro.md) evaluates pi-durable, Cua Driver and DeepSeek for an optional paid tier, including recovery, platform limits and qualification gates. It is a proposal, not an enabled feature.

Dictating text and asking the computer to act are separate intents. Dictation must never silently become a command. A future command session will have an explicit start/stop boundary and visible progress on the same phone surface. Screen capture, model credentials, observation privacy, cancellation and confirmation for consequential actions need their own implementation and validation. Untrusted text from a webpage or desktop app cannot grant additional authority to the agent.

No CUA model, cloud transcription, screen streaming or arbitrary-command endpoint is enabled by this change. The existing authenticated, certificate-pinned action channel and native receiver permission gates remain the executor boundary.

## Validation boundary

Gesture timing, focus metadata, transport and compile checks can run without moving the user’s real pointer. The [October 14 composer regression record](verification/composer-2026-10-14.md) covers the compact icon layout, explicit-send continuation and edge scrolling; its screenshots use isolated simulator fixtures. The [0.7.0 scoped physical record](verification/realtime-input-0.7.0.md) confirms iPhone-to-Windows RichEdit import, append, deletion and selection; live speech and further cases remain pending. The [0.6.0 record](verification/2026-10-13-iphone-input.md) separately covers gestures and explicit-send microphone input. Offline previews do not establish frame rate or input latency, and Reduce Motion, Typeless and broader target coverage still need their own acceptance. On the shared Mac, receiver windows and input targets must be isolated through Yomado; moving a window alone is insufficient.
